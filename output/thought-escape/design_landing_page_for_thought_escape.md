### Where the real traffic lives: portals, not owned sites

The dominant Arrow Escape experience online is not arrow-escape.com — it's **CrazyGames** (6,777 ratings, 9.2/10 score) and **Yandex Games**. These are gaming portal platforms hosting a game called "Arrow Escape" — a web-playable tile-slider puzzle with strong social proof. Users find it by searching casually, play it free in-browser, and leave. No app store, no brand relationship, no retention mechanism.

This is the actual competitive situation: Thought Escape isn't fighting other app landing pages. It's differentiating from a **browser-based casual game** with no identity and no mobile app.

### The keyword opportunity: "arrow games"

- Search volume: ~3,600 US searches/month

- 62% of traffic in the niche flows through that one ranking
- **A well-built, trust-signaling landing page from Thought Escape can rank here**

Secondary targets: "arrow puzzle game", "tile escape puzzle", "meditative puzzle game", "calm puzzle app"

The user intent behind "arrow games" is **casual discovery, not brand search** — people want to find something new to play. That makes the FAQ, feature descriptions, and first impression critical.

## Part 2: What CrazyGames Does Right (and What We Can Beat)

CrazyGames is the real benchmark because that's where the audience currently lives. Study what works there and offer something better on a owned page.

### What CrazyGames gets right
- Immediate visual: game thumbnail + rating + play count above the fold — zero ambiguity about what it is
- Social proof is numerical and specific (6,777 ratings, 9.2/10)
- One-click to play — no friction
- Genre tags that help users self-select ("Puzzle", "Arrow", "Logic")
- Comments/reviews visible — feels community-validated

### Where a dedicated app landing page beats it
- **Identity**: CrazyGames has no brand. Thought Escape has a name, a world system, a visual identity. That's a moat.
- **Mobile**: CrazyGames serves web. Thought Escape is a native app with haptics, save states, streak systems — none of which a browser game can replicate.
- **Depth signal**: A real App Store listing + a polished landing page signals "this is a serious product" vs. "this is a flash game."
- **Trust for IAP**: Users who encounter Thought Escape on a well-built landing page before downloading are more likely to trust it enough to spend. CrazyGames players have zero purchase intent.

## Part 3: Landing Page Design Recommendations

### Principle: Win on calm identity, not feature count

The arrow-escape/arrowsgo sites (to the extent they have landing pages) skew toward dense feature lists, flat color, and game-portal aesthetics. Thought Escape's differentiator is that it's built for calm — and the landing page should make that felt within 2 seconds of arrival.

---

### Above the fold

**Goal**: In under 5 seconds, a visitor knows: what this is, why it's different, and where to download it.

**Layout**:
- Background: The game's 4-world art system (forest/ocean/mountain/snow), cycling or defaulting to forest — atmospheric, dark, premium
- Headline: `"A puzzle game built for calm"` — not `"16 tile mechanics and 11 power-ups"` (that's for the features section)
- Sub-headline: One factual sentence — `"Tap arrow tiles to escape them off the board. Four themed worlds. Infinite levels."`
- CTA: Two side-by-side pill buttons — `App Store` and `Google Play` — with platform icons
- Hero visual: Phone mockup showing an in-progress level — real game screenshot, not illustration

**What to avoid**:
- Auto-playing sound or animation that feels jarring
- Star ratings you made up (earn them on the App Store first)
- Countdown timers or "limited time" urgency (undermines the calm positioning)

---

### Social proof block (below hero)

Since Thought Escape won't have thousands of App Store reviews at launch, use a different form of credibility:

- `"Designed to be meditative, not manipulative"` — as a pull quote in serif, large
- One or two short testimonials from beta users (real quotes only, with permission)
- Download count or "X levels completed" once real numbers exist
- App Store / Google Play rating badge once reviews accumulate

**Do not fabricate metrics.** The trust built by honest language compounds.

---

### Feature section: The Four Worlds

Not a feature grid — a **world explorer**. Four cards or tabs, one per world (Forest, Ocean, Mountain, Snow), each showing:
- World art background
- World name + currency name (e.g., "Forest · Spirit Seeds")
- 2–3 sentence description of the feel, not a mechanic list
- Real tile PNG at large size

Users understand the depth of the game from the art before they read a single mechanic name.

---

### Mechanics section: "How it works"

A simple 3-step horizontal flow with icons or micro-animations:

1. **Tap a tile** → arrow fires in its direction
2. **Chain escapes** → tiles push each other off the board
3. **Clear the board** → earn world currency, advance your streak

Keep this to 3 steps maximum. The game is easy to understand — overselling the complexity is a mistake.

---

### Pricing transparency (critical for IAP trust)

Given the competition is browser-free-to-play, users coming from that context have zero purchase intent. The landing page should neutralize "this is a money trap" anxiety before it forms.

Recommended block:

> **Free to download. Never pay-to-win.**
>
> Core gameplay — all campaign levels, all daily puzzles, your full streak — is free forever. Optional extras: cosmetic board skins ($0.99–$1.99), shard packs for power-up tools, and a Remove Ads upgrade if you'd prefer no ads at all. Nothing that costs money affects whether you can solve a level.

This converts skeptics. Most puzzle game landing pages bury pricing or omit it entirely, which creates anxiety. Thought Escape's pricing is genuinely fair — leading with that is a competitive advantage.

---

### FAQ section (doubles as AEO + SEO)

Keep answers short, factual, and direct. Target these questions specifically:

- **Is Thought Escape free?** Yes. Download is free. Optional in-app purchases exist for cosmetics and convenience, but all campaign and daily levels are free forever.
- **Is it available on Android?** Coming soon. iOS first.
- **How is it different from other tile puzzle games?** No forced ads between levels, no timers on standard levels, no gacha mechanics. Four themed worlds with persistent progress and a daily streak system.
- **Does it work offline?** Campaign levels and daily puzzles work fully offline. Leaderboard requires a connection.
- **What are the four worlds?** Ocean, Mountain, Forest, and Snow — each with unique art, tile materials, sound design, and its own currency.

**Why this matters for SEO/AEO**: These are real questions users type. Matching the question phrasing increases chances of appearing in AI answer boxes and featured snippets.

---

### Footer and trust signals

Every page in the site already has Privacy, Terms, and Support linked in the footer — keep that. Add:

- "Made by one person" byline (solo-built is a trust signal in this era of AI slop)
- Substack link to the build journal (signals authentic craft and ongoing development)
- No generic "we're a passionate team committed to excellence" copy

## Part 4: SEO Execution Plan

### Target pages and their jobs

| Page | Primary keyword | Intent |
|---|---|---|
| index.html | arrow games, arrow puzzle game | Discovery — user wants to play something |
| support.html | thought escape help | Brand — user already has the app |
| llms.txt | (machine-readable) | AI search — cited in ChatGPT/Perplexity answers |

### On-page signals to strengthen

1. **H1 must contain a keyword**: Current "A puzzle game built for calm" is brand positioning — strong. Consider adding a subtitle `<h2>` that contains "arrow tile puzzle" or "arrow escape game" to capture keyword intent without compromising the headline.

2. **Image alt text on hero mockup**: `alt="Thought Escape gameplay — arrow tile puzzle on a forest-themed board"` — this is indexable.

3. **JSON-LD SoftwareApplication**: Already present. Ensure `applicationCategory: "GameApplication"`, `genre: "Puzzle"`, `operatingSystem: "iOS"` are all populated.

4. **FAQ JSON-LD on index.html**: Already present. Make sure the questions match the exact phrasing people type ("is thought escape free", "what is thought escape").

5. **Internal linking**: Support → index, Privacy → index, Terms → index. All already in place via footer.

6. **Page speed**: Static HTML on Cloudflare CDN is essentially instant. Keep hero image optimized (WebP, `loading="eager"` on hero, `loading="lazy"` below fold).

## Part 5: What NOT to Build

Avoid patterns common to bad gaming landing pages:

- **Fake countdown timers** ("Download in the next 24 hours for bonus shards!") — undermines the calm brand
- **Interstitial overlays** on page load — ironic for a game that brags about no forced ads
- **Social share walls** requiring sign-in to see content — not relevant here
- **Testimonial carousels with stock photos** — use real quotes or nothing
- **"As seen in" logos without actual press coverage** — wait until you have real press
- **Chatbots or support widgets on the landing page** — adds noise, sends to support.html instead
- **Video autoplay with sound** — wrong tone entirely for a meditative game
